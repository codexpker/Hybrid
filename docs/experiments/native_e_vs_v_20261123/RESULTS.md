## What changed

The E arm reused the seed-2025 point-weighted source plan byte for byte. Each update used 16 SWaT and 16 SMD nominal reconstruction windows, plus 15 positive and 15 matched normal source endpoints from five eligible SWaT events. Four disjoint source events supplied the fixed validation. Both arms fine-tuned the original decoder from the same official weights with the original nominal per-copy reconstruction loss, the same positive-point-count weights, margin 1 and pair coefficient 1. Each endpoint came from a 100-observation input, one independent window per native forward and the same ten complementary masks.

For V, the pair score used the channel mean of the unbiased variance across ten reconstructed endpoints. For E, it used the channel mean of the squared difference between the ten-copy mean reconstruction and the observed endpoint. Both used the transform log(1 + score / a) inside the same softplus pair objective, with a separate frozen median a on the same 4,632 nominal source FIT endpoints. The source E scale was 0.3004270047. The pre-training check reproduced the archived V exactly across all 4,632 endpoints and compared E with independent float64 arithmetic. The decoder gradient norm on the checked E endpoint was 0.52524.

The E validation risk was 0.961834 at initialization, 0.860070 at the selected step 200 and 0.864492 at the 240-step cap. ## Evidence attributable to the criterion

On PSM, V supervision with the V readout exceeded E supervision with E by 0.05367 AP and 0.09027 ROC-AUC. Both improved relative to their own official initializations, E by a larger absolute amount from a much lower starting AP. E supervision followed by V reduced AP by 0.07721 from official V. At the separately calibrated suffix operating points, three-confirmation V/V matched 10 of 48 eligible events with 93 normal false fragments; E/E matched 12 with 175. Long steady macro coverage was 0.531 and 0.882 respectively.

MSL and SMAP distinguish absolute detector strength from the value of training the deployed score. V training improved V AP on each spacecraft, while E training reduced V AP. Yet E/E had higher absolute macro AP than V/V on both. The official E detector also exceeded E/E on both. MSL three-confirmation matched 24 versus 26 of 35 events for V/V versus E/E; normal false fragments were 165 versus 145. Only four events qualified for long coverage: equal-event steady coverage favored V/V (0.214 versus 0.105), whereas point-weighted steady coverage favored E/E (0.122 versus 0.172). SMAP matched 36 versus 34 of 57 events, with 808 versus 805 normal fragments and observed normal FPR 12.22% versus 7.24%. Ten long events contributed steady coverage.

On SMD, V/V gained 0.01348 mean AP relative to E/E but won only 13 of 28 machines (15 losses, median difference −0.00119). Both stayed below their own official AP, and V/V lost 0.00797 VUS-PR relative to E/E. E supervision followed by V improved ROC-AUC, VUS-PR and VUS-ROC over official V despite slightly lower AP. Thus the value of direct V training depends on the task and metric. Three-confirmation V/V produced 1,305 normal fragments versus 2,190 for E/E, while alarming on a larger fraction of normal points. One-to-one event matches were 123 versus 136 of 327, any-hit counts 158 versus 155, and long steady pooled coverage 0.698 versus 0.517. The longest missed span was 837 versus 1,204 points. [Entity-level paired differences](smd/paired.csv)

**Decision.** Retain direct V supervision as a source-supervised ranking candidate. With the deployment readout fixed to V, the matched E objective failed to reproduce the AP gain on PSM, MSL and SMAP. The same evidence gives no general advantage in absolute AP over E/E across tasks, and SMD provides a counterexample to requiring direct V supervision for VUS improvement. The backbone and FRFT computation were shared. Only the V candidate has three-seed source-training repetition evidence. The present E contrast covers one project seed and zero target updates.
